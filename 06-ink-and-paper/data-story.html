CIVIC CURRICULUM / WORK SAMPLE

Data storytelling for city staff

A 60-minute live online session that helps practitioners turn numbers into a decision someone can act on.

Use a five-part frame — Audience, Headline, Evidence, Caveat, Ask — on a real city decision, then on your own data.

INSIDE THIS SAMPLEDesign rationaleSession packageObjectives, practice and evidenceThe real city exampleAdult learning in practice

Design rationale

The learner
City staff collect a lot of data, but numbers alone rarely move a council vote, a budget request or a resident conversation. Participants range across levels and need no statistics background.
The approach
Teach one repeatable frame, model it on a sourced WWC city example (Boulder, Colorado), then practice on a short dataset in breakouts with peer feedback.
The intended outcome
Each participant leaves with a draft headline and ask for a story from their own work, plus a worksheet they can reuse as a template.

Session package

Facilitator guide (PDF, 8 pp.)Participant worksheet (PDF, 4 pp.)
  1. AudienceWho decides, and what do they care about?
  2. HeadlineThe one sentence you want them to remember.
  3. EvidenceOne to three numbers and one chart that prove it.
  4. CaveatWhat the data can’t tell us yet.
  5. AskThe specific decision or next step you want.

Objectives, practice and evidence

The real city example

Boulder’s Eviction Prevention and Rental Assistance Services program offers legal services, rental assistance and mediation. WWC reports that participants had a 96% eviction-prevention rate, compared with 58% for non-participants, and that the city added $253,000 to the program after combining that evaluation with resident feedback. Sources: WWC Boulder city profile; WWC, “Four Cities Prove That Certification Isn’t the Destination” (2025).

Built-in caveat practice: the comparison is between people who chose to use services and people who didn’t, so it shows association, not proof that the program caused the difference. Boulder’s own 2025 budget summary describes the added program funding as $315,000, so facilitators should check the current figure before quoting it. Spotting that kind of gap is exactly the “Caveat” step.
