CIVIC CURRICULUM / WORK SAMPLE

Seattle: contracting for resident outcomes

A sourced historical case from What Works Cities and its implementation partner.

Use a documented city practice to teach the difference between buying activity and managing for outcomes.

INSIDE THIS SAMPLEDesign rationaleWhat Seattle changedClaim-to-source registerTwelve-minute applicationProposed curriculum supportsOfficial practice connectionSources and historical scope

Design rationale

The learner
An inspiring city story is only useful if a practitioner can see what changed and what evidence supports it.
The approach
Cross-check the official WWC narrative against the implementation partner’s 2016 report; distinguish documented changes from teaching proposals.
The intended outcome
A historical case, application exercise and two curriculum-support hypotheses.

What Seattle changed

In August 2015, Seattle’s Mayor’s Office, Human Services Department and five providers began a homelessness-services contracting pilot with GPL support through WWC. HSD consolidated 26 contracts into eight portfolio contracts, totaling $8.5 million annually. [1, pp. 1–2]

The pilot standardized outcome and process measures, used historical data to inform targets and introduced regular performance conversations. Contract consolidation was intended to reduce administration and allow more flexible resource use. [1]

The later WWC narrative, prepared for Seattle’s 2018 certification, describes a shift from counting activity, such as beds filled, toward outcomes such as moving into permanent housing and avoiding returns to homelessness. [2]

Download Seattle case & curriculum memo (PDF)

Claim-to-source register

No percentage reduction in homelessness or budget reallocation is asserted. The target to double housing moves in the WWC narrative is not treated as an achieved result. Appendix A of the GPL report uses illustrative numbers and is not used as outcome evidence.

Twelve-minute application

  1. 2 minutes: identify an output and a resident outcome in the case.
  2. 4 minutes: draft a performance-review question that invites problem-solving.
  3. 4 minutes: name a contract owner, a measure definition and a privacy or data-quality check.
  4. 2 minutes: compare with the criteria: output/outcome distinction, responsible owner, appropriate data check.
Suggested response

Occupied beds is an output; remaining housed is an outcome. Ask: “What is preventing participants from moving into stable housing, and which change can we test within the contract’s rules?” Before requesting records, confirm lawful access, minimum necessary fields and missing-data risks. This is an authored teaching example, not a Seattle quotation.

Proposed curriculum supports

Outcome-definition worksheet
Specify population, numerator, denominator, reporting period, baseline and whose experience may be missing. A second reviewer should be able to calculate the measure consistently.
Constructive review agenda
Prepare one trend, one data-quality question, one provider explanation and one action with an owner and review date.

These are design hypotheses, not findings that the WWC library lacks these tools. Validate them through a dated resource inventory and user feedback before prioritizing development.

Official practice connection

Results-Driven Contracting: “Using Data to Manage Contracts and Improve Outcomes and Performance.” Data Management: “Sharing Data” and “Protecting Data Privacy and Confidentiality.” These use the official assessment page’s names rather than unverified criterion codes. This is an instructional connection, not a certification judgment. [3]
